Private credit is becoming a two-speed market. One regime prices each transaction through negotiation, human judgment and the memory of the last comparable deal. The other regime prices continuously, from live data, with the quote updated before the counterparty finishes the sentence.

The first regime is the legacy market. The second regime is the standard forming now. The gap between them is the largest mispricing surface in institutional credit.

## The Transaction Regime

The transaction regime prices every facility as a unique event. The lender assembles comparables, applies judgment to the differences and quotes a spread. The quote is a snapshot of one moment, and it expires the moment the negotiation moves.

This regime has a structural cost. Every quote requires assembly, and assembly takes time.

The lender can price a handful of facilities per week at institutional quality, and the pipeline is the bottleneck. Deal flow waits on the pricing capacity of senior people.

The information problem is worse than the capacity problem. Transaction pricing captures the state of the market at the moment of negotiation, and the moment is always stale. The comparables are lagging indicators, and the judgment applied to them carries the bias of the last deal that went wrong.

## The Continuous Regime

The continuous regime prices from a live model. Collateral values, cash flow data, registry events and market spreads feed a pricing engine that quotes in real time. The facility is repriced on every material input change, and the spread follows the asset, not the negotiation.

The economics invert. The marginal cost of a quote approaches zero because the infrastructure does the assembly.

The lender prices thousands of facilities with the same senior capacity that the transaction regime spends on a handful. The bottleneck moves from pricing to sourcing, and sourcing is a machine problem.

The information advantage compounds. Every repriced facility produces a data point, and every data point sharpens the model. The continuous lender accumulates a pricing history that no transaction lender can reconstruct, because the transaction lender never marked the position between deals.

## Why the Split Happens Now

The two regimes have coexisted for decades, with the transaction regime dominant. The split accelerates now because the inputs for continuous pricing reached critical mass. Registry data is digitized, collateral is telemetric, and the infrastructure layer that connects them is agentic.

The agentic layer is the difference. A model that quotes from a spreadsheet is a tool. A system that monitors registries, ingests asset telemetry, detects covenant drift and updates the quote without human intervention is a market maker.

The first requires a human to run it. The second runs the human out of the loop.

The cost curve decides the outcome. Transaction pricing scales linearly with senior headcount.

Continuous pricing scales with compute, and compute falls in price on a schedule. The regime with the falling cost curve absorbs the volume, and the other regime retreats to the assets that resist standardization.

## The Standard Forms at the Margin

Markets do not switch regimes by decree. They switch at the margin, one facility class at a time. The standard forms where the data is cleanest and the volume is highest: receivables, inventory, equipment, and the asset-backed products that already price against collateral.

Each class that converts pulls the neighboring class with it. The lender that prices receivables continuously builds the infrastructure to price inventory, and the infrastructure generalizes. The pricing engine is the wedge, and the wedge moves through the asset classes in order of data quality.

The conversion has a second-order effect. As continuous pricing captures a class, the transaction quotes in that class lose their reference function.

Buyers and sellers stop comparing against negotiation prices because the negotiation prices are no longer the market. The continuous quote becomes the benchmark, and the benchmark reinforces the regime.

## What the Split Does to Origination

The two-speed market rewrites the economics of origination. In the transaction regime, origination and pricing are separate functions with separate incentives.

The originator sources the deal, and the pricer decides whether it clears. The handoff costs time, and time is mispricing.

In the continuous regime, origination is an input to the pricing engine. The advisor channel, the registry signal and the referral feed become data streams that the engine prices the moment they arrive. The deal is quoted before it is packaged, and the quote decides which deals advance.

This is the systems mandate in operation. The operator who owns the pricing engine and the origination feed owns the spread. The operator who owns only one side of the system competes against someone who owns both, and the two-sided operator prices the one-sided operator out of every clean asset class.

## The Counterparty Shift

Continuous pricing changes the counterparty conversation. A lender with a live quote does not negotiate from a blank page. The counterparty receives a price that reflects the current state of the collateral, the market and the lender's book, and the negotiation shifts from price discovery to structure.

The shift favors speed. The counterparty who accepts the continuous quote closes in days.

The counterparty who negotiates against it discovers that the quote moves while the negotiation runs, because the engine reprices on every input. The negotiation premium becomes a cost, and the market prices it.

The dynamic concentrates volume in the continuous channel. Borrowers with clean assets and time pressure choose the lender who quotes instantly. The transaction channel retains the complex, the opaque and the slow, and adverse selection thickens in the regime that already carries the higher cost.

## The Edge Cases

Continuous pricing fails where the data fails. A facility against an asset with no telemetry, no registry and no comparable history resists the engine, and the transaction regime keeps the mandate. The boundary between the regimes is a data boundary, not a sophistication boundary.

The operator prices the boundary explicitly. The engine quotes the classes it can see, and the human prices the classes it cannot. The hybrid is the institutional answer, and the ratio between machine-priced and human-priced volume is the operating metric.

The jurisdiction dimension decides the pace. Markets with digitized registries and electronic collateral administration convert first. Markets with paper perfection and manual filing lag, and the lender who operates across both captures the arbitrage while the lag persists.

## The Mandate

The two-speed market is not a transition. It is the equilibrium of the next decade, with the continuous regime absorbing volume and the transaction regime retreating to the data-poor margin.

The operator who builds the pricing engine, the origination feed and the cross-border execution layer inherits the standard. The operator who waits for the market to settle inherits the adverse selection.

## Summary

Private credit splits into a transaction regime and a continuous regime, and the continuous regime becomes the standard where data is clean. The pricing engine absorbs origination, the advisor channel becomes a data stream and the quote becomes the benchmark.

Operators who own the full system capture the spread, and operators who price by negotiation retreat to the data-poor margin. The boundary between the regimes is a data boundary, and it moves in favor of the machine.
