The lender who priced a loan yesterday and the lender who prices it this afternoon operate in different markets. The second one has a data layer. For most of the private credit industry, that layer has been the missing component: the market runs on call reports, portal snapshots and the memory of the relationship manager. In 2026, the infrastructure is being built, and it changes where origination advantage comes from.

The build is visible in the money. Versana, an industry-backed data platform for the syndicated loan and private credit markets, closed a 43 million USD round in April 2026 led by BNP Paribas, with Fitch Ventures and MassMutual Ventures participating, roughly 473 million SEK at current rates. The company's stated scope is the roughly 8 trillion USD broadly syndicated loan and private credit market, and in May 2026 it extended its digital data offering to trade and standby letters of credit. The direction is unambiguous: the loan market is becoming a data market.

## Real-Time Agent Data

The operational core of the new layer is what Versana calls real-time digital agent data: the loan-level information that agents, the banks administering facilities, hold about borrowers. That data has historically been private to each agent bank, released in monthly statements and reconstructed by lenders through manual collection. Centralised and digitised, it becomes a shared feed that every lender sees at the same time.

The consequence for origination is structural. A lender with access to current facility data can see a borrower's covenant headroom, utilization and payment behavior before the borrower starts a new financing conversation. The information that used to arrive through relationship channels now arrives through the feed. The advisor channel still matters for the introduction. The data layer decides who is prepared when the introduction happens.

## The Pricing Consequence

The data layer feeds the pricing layer. Per Advisor Perspectives' May 2026 report, Apollo announced that it will soon provide daily pricing for its private credit funds, a move the publication describes as potentially transformative for the asset class. Daily marks require daily data, and daily data requires the infrastructure being built now. The two developments are the same development: the market is moving from periodic valuation to continuous pricing.

Continuous pricing changes the competitive field. A platform that reprices its book daily can respond to a new mandate with a current spread instead of a stale one. Per Octus' Americas Private Credit 2026 outlook, tighter spreads and the indirect effect of rate cuts should boost refinancing activity, which means more borrowers in the market comparing offers. The borrower with a live data picture and the lender with live pricing meet at a different negotiating table than the one the industry used in 2023.

## What This Does to Origination

Origination becomes a preparation game. The lender who monitors the loan-level feed sees the refinancing trigger before the teaser exists: the maturity approaching, the covenant tightening, the utilization climbing. The response is not a cold call. It is a prepared conversation with a current structure, because the data made the preparation possible.

The advisor channel compounds the effect. The CPA or attorney who hears about the borrower's plans still provides the introduction. But the introduction lands differently when the lender arrives with facility-level knowledge and a live price. The advisor's information advantage narrows, and the lender's preparation advantage widens. The referral network protocol of 2025 becomes the data-enabled channel of 2026.

## The Human Gate

The system still runs on human judgment. Loan data shows what the borrower has done, not what the borrower will do, and underwriting remains the test that no feed replaces. The infrastructure removes the information collection problem, which is real, and leaves the credit decision problem, which is harder. The firms that built manual collection empires lose that advantage. The firms that built judgment on top of the new data gain the same position.

The limitation deserves precision. Centralized data reduces the gap between lenders on facility-level facts. It does not reduce the gap on relationships, sector knowledge or restructuring capability. A lender with the feed and no sector expertise still loses to a lender with sector expertise and a slower feed. The data layer is necessary, not sufficient, and the firms that treat it as the whole game will discover the distinction at underwriting.

## The Standardization Endgame

The endgame is standardization. When agent data flows through a common infrastructure, the loan market acquires the property that public markets have had for decades: a shared reference picture that every participant sees. The reference picture does not eliminate dispersion. It moves the dispersion to the parts of the market where it belongs, pricing, structure and judgment, instead of leaving it in the data.

The capital is already voting. 43 million USD from bank-led investors, an 8 trillion USD addressable market, daily pricing announcements and a product expansion into letters of credit: the components of a new market infrastructure are being assembled in public. The firms that wire their origination systems to the data layer while it is being built will have the preparation advantage when the layer becomes the baseline. The firms that wait for the baseline will buy the infrastructure at its full price, in data quality, in pricing capability and in deals.

## Summary

Loan-market data infrastructure is being built in public. Versana's 43 million USD raise and its expansion into letters of credit, Apollo's move to daily pricing and the industry's shift toward continuous data all point in the same direction: origination advantage is moving from information collection to information use. The lender with real-time facility data and a live price arrives at the mandate prepared. The human gate at underwriting remains, and the firms that combine the feed with judgment will own the position the manual collectors are losing.
