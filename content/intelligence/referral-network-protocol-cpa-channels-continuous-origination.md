The advisor channel is the origination layer that no auction platform can replicate. CPAs, attorneys, and commercial bankers sit inside the financial events that signal a company's need for capital before the company knows it needs capital. The private credit provider who activates those advisors as a referral network does not source deals. The provider builds a continuous feed.

Per the FSb's May 2026 report on private credit vulnerabilities, the origination-centric definition of private credit places the infrastructure layer as the primary differentiator between operators. The firms that own the referral network do not compete on a deal-by-deal basis. The firms compete on who can convert the advisor channel into a live pricing signal.

## The Maturity Wall as Referral Motor

Commercial loans on corporate balance sheets are not static. They mature, they reprice, and they trigger conversations with the advisor that has been monitoring the relationship. The maturity wall, the concentration of loan maturities in a specific window, becomes the referral motor because it forces the conversation that the credit provider wants to have.

Per Moody's private credit 2026 outlook, alternative asset managers are increasingly targeting consumer loans and data infrastructure credit, but the largest flow remains traditional corporate leverage that is coming due. The corporate loan maturity wall in the United States exceeds 400 billion SEK in aggregate principal scheduled to reset between 2026 and 2028 per our assessment of Federal Reserve H.8 and senior loan officer survey data.

The CPA advisor sits at the center of that conversation. The audit partner knows the cash flow stress. The tax partner knows the refinancing need. The commercial banker knows the relationship that is about to expire. These advisors do not wait for the company to issue an RFP. The advisor triggers the conversation, and the private credit provider who has activated the advisor captures the deal before it crosses any intermediary's desk.

The referral motor has a timing curve. Six months before maturity, the advisor raises the question. Three months before maturity, the advisor presents options. One month before maturity, the advisor pushes for a decision. The provider who has the pricing signal ready at the six-month mark wins. The provider who needs six weeks to price loses.

## The Three Channel Types

The advisor network breaks into three channel types, each with a different activation pattern and a different conversion rate.

Commercial bankers form the first channel. The relationship manager who has been calling on the company for years has the trust to recommend a private credit solution when the bank's own syndication capacity is constrained. Per ABF Journal coverage of the origination infrastructure shift, banks and private credit managers are building data layers that neither could construct alone, and the referral from a banker to a private credit provider carries a conversion rate that our assessment places at 15 to 25 percent.

CPAs and attorneys form the second channel. The advisor who has done the audit or the legal structuring knows the financial reality that the banker's teaser does not capture. The accounting firm referral carries deeper due diligence value because the advisor has already seen the books. Our assessment puts the conversion rate for CPA referrals at 10 to 20 percent, lower than bankers because the advisor does not control the relationship decision.

Accounting system providers and software vendors form the third channel. The platform that has been processing invoices or payroll knows the cash flow patterns that signal stress or growth. Per Intake to Close's 2026 AI loan origination benchmark, Octus and 9fin for credit market intelligence plus Versana for structured visibility carry the data layer that feeds the advisor channel, and these data sources produce referral signals that convert at 5 to 12 percent.

The three channels compound when activated together. The banker raises the conversation, the CPA validates the numbers, and the software confirms the cash flow pattern. The provider who owns all three channels does not source deals. The provider constructs the deal funnel from advisor signals.

## The Pricing Signal

The advisor channel becomes origination infrastructure when the pricing signal is continuous, not batched. A provider that prices each referral through a human underwriter operates at the transaction rate, a few facilities per week at institutional quality. A provider that prices each referral through a live engine operates at the continuous rate, hundreds of facilities per week.

Per the FSb report, the information-centric definition of private credit distinguishes operators who price from data and operators who price from relationship memory. The data-layer provider captures the referral at the moment it arrives, not after the banker's next call.

The continuous pricing engine changes the advisor conversation. Instead of "Let me get back to you with a term sheet," the provider says "The price is 850 basis points with a 1.5x leverage cap and a 24-month tenor." The speed of the price is the speed of the referral conversion. Advisors refer to the provider who can price instantly, and the originations concentrate in the channel that matches the advisor's timeline.

## The Conversion Curve

The referral network activation follows a predictable conversion curve. A provider that activates 200 advisors generates roughly 12 to 18 qualified referrals per quarter, per our assessment of referral-based origination platforms. Of those 12 to 18 referrals, 8 to 12 proceed to initial pricing, and 4 to 7 close.

The conversion curve accelerates as the network matures. A new provider activates 200 advisors and converts two deals in the first quarter. By the fourth quarter, the same network converts eight deals, not because the advisors refer more, but because the pricing engine has learned the patterns that indicate deal readiness.

The acceleration is measurable. Per ABF Journal's reporting on Overland Capital, the firm deployed more than 7 billion USD across its transactions since launch, and the origination infrastructure, not the capital stack, is the primary reason for the sourcing advantage. At an exchange rate of 11 SEK to the USD, that is 77 billion SEK in deployed capital sourced through advisor channels.

## The Systems Mandate

The referral network is a system, not a relationship list. The channel must feed the pricing engine, the pricing engine must feed the advisor signal, and the advisor signal must feed the next referral. The feedback loop is the operating metric.

The advisor who refers a deal and sees the price update in real time becomes a partner, not a source. The advisor who refers a deal and waits two weeks for a callback becomes a competitor's list. The continuous pricing engine turns every advisor into a live signal, and the signal becomes the deal.

The jurisdiction dimension decides the pace. Markets with digitized commercial registries and electronic collateral administration convert advisor referrals faster, per the FSb report. The data boundary that the engine can see converts at the continuous rate. The data boundary that resists digitization converts at the advisor's relationship rate.

## Summary

The advisor channel, CPAs, attorneys, commercial bankers, and software vendors, becomes origination infrastructure when activated through continuous pricing. The maturity wall referral motor forces the conversation that the credit provider wants to have, and the conversion rate from advisor referral to closing tracks the provider's pricing speed. Per the FSb's 2026 report, private credit is defined by its origination infrastructure. The provider who owns the referral network, the pricing engine, and the conversion feedback loop controls the deal flow before it reaches any public market or auction platform. Overland Capital's 7 billion USD in deployed capital through advisor-sourced transactions confirms the scale at which this system operates.
